* [[id:ed1a7a1f-dbb5-445f-b451-b447a2b9a230][LEMR]] Application Requirements Assessment
*** /A patient logging support system/
- Core Functional Requirements
  - A _Staff_ member should be able to simply open an account for an individual with "whatever" information they are able to get from an Individual 
  - A _Staff_ member should principally be able to look up an _Individual_ via a _LEMR QR Code_
  - The system must allow clinicians (users) to search for and retrieve patient (person) records using natural language or descriptive queries—even without a specific patient identifier or QR code. For example, a doctor should be able to search for a patient by recalling contextual details such as age, medical history, or prior diagnoses.
  - The software should feature robust patient lookup functionality that is superior to current paper and ledger-based systems, making clinicians prefer this digital solution over existing methods.
- Technical and Data Requirements
  - The platform needs to operate as an internet-based application, easily accessible to medical professionals who might lack traditional EHRs (Electronic Health Records).
  - Data should be accessible, exportable, and potentially monetizable—supporting structured collection suitable for selling medical data points, e.g., to startups purchasing data from African hospitals.
  - Security and privacy considerations are important, especially as there is discussion of NDAs and non-competes; the system should be audit-capable and compliant with relevant privacy regulations.
  - Self-hosting on a Linux server should be supported, accommodating organizations that prefer local control over data and minimizing dependency on third parties.
- User Experience and Adoption Requirements
  - Usability is critical: the interface should support clinicians’ workflow, including easy patient lookups and minimal friction compared to analog methods.
  - There should be flexibility to update or change contact and notification settings (such as for emails or phone numbers), for user manageability.
  - The software must track and audit usage in a way that is at least as trustworthy as or better than paper records, giving confidence to both clinicians and administrators.
- Business and Stakeholder Considerations
  - The system’s business model should account for sales or licensing based on data points (e.g., $1 per data point), with features that streamline legal protections like NDAs for organizational users.
  - The terminology choice ("user" vs. "person") should remain adaptable at both surface and system levels, to protect the project and stakeholders legally.
  - The software should aim to fill a clear gap: digitizing and making accessible patient records for organizations relying on paper, Excel, or Word docs—nothing like this currently exists for internet-based use in the target regions.
These requirements are synthesized from the key themes, needs, and concerns expressed in the correspondence. They should provide a solid foundation for both feature brainstorming and the technical design of the LEMR Medical Social Support System.
