* [[id:b42ab017-a31b-4742-bb11-6739d926fae3][Prompt]] for [[id:67889323-a4c5-48d8-91eb-d44995ce8dff][Ableton Live]] Collections Organization
- Context
  - Ableton Live 12, Mac OS 15
- TASK
  - Come up with 4 ways to name and use the 7 Collections "tabs" for maximum organization, and intuitive access. Each of the 4 ways should be of different levels of creative, from more traditional, to outside the box. 
  - Include notes on sub folders for each
- Background
  - I could use some help thinking outside the box here. I am trying to simplify my Ableton live setup, routine, and music making process. I love the free form aspect of Ableton, but it feels a little cluttered lately, and I want to streamline it.
  - I wanted to have the 7 color coded collections as my gateway to the things I need in my creation process. Generally people will use these to save their favorite presets, possibly for different styles of instrument, samples in different ways, most folks use a whole heep of preset categories. Personally I think there is a neat way to do it that I am not thinking of. Perhaps just a couple tweaks.
  - For me there are always conceptual issues based on which TYPE of criteria. If I have a Collection called "Instruments", then is this Instrument plugins? instrument patches (ableton racks in my case), which are associated with plugins? Do I Sort the patches by frequency, instrument, style?
  - One thing I do like to do is only add folders to the collections. This way I have two levels of categorization, and the contents will update automatically when things are added or changed. I try not to go too much deeper than one level, though there will probable be a few instances where I have /Collection/Folder/Sub-Folder for categorization. 
  - These are the type of "Things" that I generally use in ableton, in order for how frequently I am digging for them. for most I will include two numbers. the first is how many sub divisions of each thing there might be, which could probebly translate to sub folders, but not necessarily. And the second number would be a guess on the absolute total. These are off the top of my head.
  - List of Music Things I use
    - Drum Samples
      - 8 categories
      - 1000s total
    - Drum Racks
      - I'm trying to move from selecting samples all the time, because it's time consuming and kills your ears. Do some predefined but mildly versitile drum racks.
      - just a handful total
    - Instrument Plugins
      - 7 total
      - no category split
      - It used to be a lot more, I'm on my way down, and might limit it to just Diva or Serum 2, because they both can cover most bases
    - Effect Plugins
      - 11 categories
      - 60-70 total
      - I rarely go for "presets" here for EQs and Compressors. I treat them like I would in the studio. However for organization, I general drag the "default" setting for the plugin into 
    - Instrument Racks 
      - This is how i generally group my individual sounds, as I use several instrument plugins, and I try not to let the source limit me, and there are usually external plugins for the sound.
      - these I generally have split into different styles like, bass, mid, lead, pad. 
      - there are also 3rd party presets, sampled instruments, tempate patches. 
      - 10 categories
      - 120+ total
    - Drum Loops 
      - Sampled audio drum loops. I have a ton, and like to build beats around them
      - theres bunches that have been imported from sample libraries. I might move away from these
      - I am creating lots of drum loops from my previous lots of drum kit building. Practical loops like riding on the high hats n stuff. 
        - I'm not sure how much to just dump my own drum loops into files, or bother with clips (see below) for these. I'd rather not be reduntant, as to simplify and not be precious. 
    - Utility sessions
      - These would be saved groups of tracks, for different kinds of sessions. If I'm recording for a rock song for instance, this will be like 2 audio tracks for bass, 4 audio tracks for rhythm guitar. And I might have a separate sessions preset for 6 or 8 vocal tracks.
      - These save me time, and are color coded. Sometimes I remove the group track once I drag the whole thing into the current project. 
    - Clips
      - As a simplification process, I was thinking about moving toward creating a lot of interchangable clips in ableton, especially for drum parts, but also bass loops, and lead parts, and little ideas. But I'm not sure how far to categorize these. Like for drums I have a top section, a snare section, a kick section, a full kick section. I Want to bounce as many to audio as possible to make it more limited, more commited, artistic. but there are also clips of drum racks and instrument racks that are editable, so do I split those up or is it not worth it?
      - not sure how deep to categorize, and they are mostly yet to be made. 
    - Record Crate
      - this is a folder of hi-fi tracks from actual albums, for stem splitting, sampling and referencing
      - 10 categories
      - 500 total
    - Resample bin
      - This is a new concept but I was gonna put a lot of the tings I resample into a general resample bin, so I know these are bits I've (relatively) recently bounced down, either for a specific project, or in a specific sub category. 
      - For instance I was just rummaging through records, stem splitting them, and then grabbing drum loops and drum samples from there. Rather than dump them into the sea of samples in my massive "drum loops" or "drum samples", I thought I'd keep them separate and relavent in this folder
    - Utility plugins
      - This is what I call like.. midi racks, racks for push 2 triggers
    - Effect Racks
      - mostly premade for creative modulation, with mapped macros
      - just a handful
  - I'm sure I'm forgetting something, or there are other categories of things that can divide these up differently. I'm open to whatever.
