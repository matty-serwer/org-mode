* Perplexity Research for [[id:ed1a7a1f-dbb5-445f-b451-b447a2b9a230][LEMR]] [[id:e056c9d6-0ada-43d3-b0b2-0f9bef20cc60][Data Types]] ([[id:2bfd8ca3-1334-4985-91d7-8215bdb86cac][Typescripy Interface]]), Functional Concerns
Based on my research into humanitarian medical missions, emergency medical records systems, and QR code-based patient tracking, I've compiled comprehensive recommendations for your application. Let me break this down into detailed sections:
** 1. Additional Features, Functions & Safety Recommendations
*** ✅ *Validated Feature Ideas*
*A. Offline-First Architecture (CRITICAL)*
- Research shows humanitarian settings often have *zero internet connectivity*
- The app MUST function completely offline with periodic syncing when internet becomes available
- Store all data locally first, sync to cloud when connection detected
- This is the #1 reason similar apps fail in the field
*B. Tiered Data Entry System*
- *Quick Registration Mode*: Minimal fields (name, age, gender, chief complaint, urgency level)
- *Standard Mode*: Core medical data (allergies, medications, conditions)
- *Comprehensive Mode*: Full SOAP notes and detailed history
- This addresses Aaron's concern about cumbersome entry during high-volume emergencies
*C. Photo Attachment with Compression*
- Despite limited internet, photos are EXTREMELY valuable for:
  - Patient identification in low-literacy populations
  - Visual documentation of wounds, rashes, conditions
  - Verification across language barriers
- Solution: Heavy compression (reduce file size 80-90%), store locally, optional cloud sync
- Research shows this is used successfully in similar humanitarian apps
*D. Medical Translation Support*
- Pre-loaded common medical phrases in major humanitarian languages (Arabic, French, Spanish, Haitian Creole, etc.)
- Symptom/diagnosis terminology in multiple languages
- Visual symptom charts for non-verbal communication
*E. Treatment & Medication Tracking*
- Record what was administered, dosage, date/time
- Flag drug interactions based on known allergies
- Track incomplete treatment courses (critical for antibiotics)
*F. Handoff/Transfer Notes*
- Specific section for "Transfer Instructions" when patient moves between zones
- Required fields: Destination, Reason for Transfer, Critical Actions Needed
- WHO Emergency Medical Team standards require this
*G. SOAP Note Templates*
- Pre-structured SOAP (Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan) format
- This is the international standard used in emergency medicine
- Makes notes consistent across different providers
*H. Triage Priority System*
- Visual color-coded urgency levels (Red=Immediate, Yellow=Urgent, Green=Delayed, Black=Deceased)
- Follows START triage system used in disaster medicine
- Helps in mass casualty situations
*I. Medication Allergy Warnings*
- Prominently display known allergies at top of profile
- Pop-up alert when entering medications if allergy match detected
*J. Immunization Tracking*
- Record vaccinations given during visit
- Critical for disease outbreak prevention in humanitarian settings
*** ⚠️ *Critical Concerns & Solutions*
*CONCERN 1: QR Code Data Embedding*
#+begin_quote
"Perhaps a QR Code would literally have information built into it"
#+end_quote
*Analysis*: Technically feasible but NOT recommended as primary solution
- QR codes can store ~4,000 alphanumeric characters max
- Medical records quickly exceed this (especially with multiple visits)
- *Problem*: If patient loses paper, data is completely gone
- *Problem*: Can't update QR code after printed
*SOLUTION*: Hybrid approach
- QR code contains: *Unique Patient ID + Basic Emergency Info*
- Emergency info embedded: Name, Age, Blood Type, Critical Allergies, Emergency Contact
- Full medical record stored in app database, retrieved via Patient ID
- This way: QR works offline for ID lookup, critical info readable even if database unavailable
*CONCERN 2: Patient Authentication*
#+begin_quote
"Any verification would be too difficult to enforce"
#+end_quote
*Analysis*: You're correct - traditional authentication won't work
- Patients may be unconscious, children, non-literate, or don't speak local language
- No consistent ID documents in humanitarian crises
*SOLUTION*: Zero-Knowledge Patient Access
- NO password/authentication required to VIEW patient data via QR scan
- Anyone with QR can instantly see medical info (this is intentional - saves lives)
- *Security through obscurity*: Only people with physical QR card can access
- Patient can't "log in" - they only possess the QR card
*CONCERN 3: Data Privacy & Security*
*SOLUTION*: Tiered Access Control for Medical Staff
- *Doctors MUST authenticate* with password/biometric to:
  - Create new patient profiles
  - Edit existing patient data
  - Print QR codes
  - Export/share data
- Track which doctor made which changes (audit log)
- Encrypt data at rest and in transit
- Comply with HIPAA-equivalent humanitarian standards
*CONCERN 4: Duplicate Patient Records*
- Risk: Same patient scanned by different doctors = multiple profiles
*SOLUTION*:
- Photo-matching assistance (optional AI flagging of similar photos)
- Search before creating: Name + approximate age + gender
- Merge duplicate profiles function
- Each QR code has globally unique identifier (UUID)
*CONCERN 5: Data Loss*
- Phone lost/broken = patient data gone?
*SOLUTION*:
- Automatic backup to secure cloud when internet available
- Doctor can access their data from any device (login to account)
- Patient QR codes work across all devices (ID is universal)
*CONCERN 6: Battery & Device Failure*
- In field, can't always charge devices
*SOLUTION*:
- Extremely battery-efficient offline mode
- Print backup summary sheets with QR code (human-readable key info)
*** 🆕 *Additional Feature Recommendations*
*K. Follow-Up Scheduling*
- Flag patients needing return visit
- Set reminder dates
- Track who didn't return (lost to follow-up)
*L. Chronic Disease Management*
- Special profiles for diabetes, hypertension, HIV/AIDS
- Track key metrics over time (blood sugar, blood pressure, CD4 counts)
*M. Vaccination Campaign Mode*
- Bulk processing mode for mass vaccination events
- Quick scan, verify eligibility, record vaccination, next patient
*N. Multi-Language UI*
- App interface in multiple languages for doctors from different countries
*O. Offline Map Integration*
- Pre-download maps of refugee camp/operational area
- Tag patient location for outbreak tracking
*P. Report Generation*
- Daily summary: # patients seen, conditions treated, supplies used
- Required by WHO Emergency Medical Team standards
- Export to PDF/CSV for reporting to coordination centers
*Q. Supply Tracking Integration*
- Reduce medication inventory when dispensed to patient
- Alert when supplies running low

  // Assessment - Provider's diagnosis
  assessment: AssessmentData;
  
  // Plan - Treatment plan

  isSynced: boolean; // Has this been backed up to cloud?

** Summary of Key Design Decisions
*** ✅ *Why This Schema Works for Humanitarian Settings:*
1. *Flexible Demographics*: Accounts for unknown dates of birth, single names, lack of formal addresses
1. *Offline-First*: All data structures designed to work without internet
1. *SOAP Note Structure*: International standard, familiar to doctors worldwide
1. *Minimal QR Data*: Only critical emergency info embedded, full record in database
1. *Photo Support*: Compressed images for identification and clinical documentation
1. *Audit Trail*: Track who did what when for accountability
1. *Transfer Documentation*: Special focus on patient handoffs between facilities
1. *Triage System*: Built-in emergency prioritization
1. *WHO Compliance*: Follows Emergency Medical Team Minimum Dataset standards
1. *Extensible*: Custom fields allow adaptation to specific mission needs
*** 🎯 *Implementation Priority (MVP)*
*Phase 1 (Weeks 1-4):*
- User authentication
- Basic patient registration (name, age, gender, photo)
- QR code generation and scanning
- Single encounter (chief complaint, basic vitals, assessment, plan)
- Offline storage
*Phase 2 (Weeks 5-8):*
- Full SOAP notes
- Medication/allergy tracking
- Triage system
- Multiple encounters per patient
- Cloud backup/sync
*Phase 3 (Weeks 9-12):*
- Transfer notes
- Immunization tracking
- Advanced search/filtering
- Reporting dashboard
- Multi-language support
This application could genuinely save lives in humanitarian crises. The key is keeping the MVP simple while building on a foundation that scales to comprehensive medical records. Happy to discuss specific implementation details or technical architecture questions!
