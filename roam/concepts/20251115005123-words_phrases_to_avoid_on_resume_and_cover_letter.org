#+title: Words & Phrases to Avoid on Resume and Cover Letter
#+filetags: :concept:
#+date: 2025-11-15
* Words & Phrases to Avoid on [[id:8fee8dc9-268c-4ee9-9572-4da77ef34489][Resume]] and [[id:8d50a124-82a1-4631-8f3a-dd5f5deb6a68][Cover Letter]]
-
  - [[id:043278c0-6df3-46a0-9235-8334fa765738][11/14/2025]]
- THE ABSOLUTE WORST OFFENDERS
  - *Self-Descriptive Buzzwords (Empty Claims)*
    - Team player
    - Results-driven / Results-oriented
    - Detail-oriented
    - Hard worker / Hardworking
    - Self-motivated / Highly motivated
    - Go-getter
    - Passionate
    - Out-of-the-box thinker
    - Dynamic
    - Innovative / Innovation-minded
    - People person
    - Problem solver
    - Fast learner / Quick learner
    - Proven track record
- VAGUE SKILL CLAIMS
  - *Communication & Interpersonal*
    - Strong communication skills
    - Excellent written and verbal communication
    - Great communicator
    - Interpersonal skills
  - *Work Style Descriptors*
    - Works well under pressure
    - Works well independently
    - Multitasker
    - Flexible
    - Adaptable
- MEANINGLESS BUSINESS JARGON
  - Synergy
  - Think outside the box
  - Best of breed
  - Best in class
  - Strategic thinker
  - Bottom-line focused
  - Value-added
  - Leverage
  - Touch base
  - Circle back
  - Deep dive
- EMPTY LEADERSHIP CLAIMS
  - Natural leader
  - Leadership skills
  - Proven leader
  - Born leader
- OVERUSED ACTION PHRASES
  - Responsible for...
  - Duties included...
  - Tasked with...
  - Helped with...
  - Worked on...
- USELESS FILLER
  - Proficient in Microsoft Office (unless you're truly advanced with Excel/Access)
  - Expert (without proof)
  - Specialist
  - Professional (redundant)
  - Experienced (show it, don't say it)
  - Guru / Ninja / Rockstar / Wizard
  - Dedicated
  - Seasoned
- SUBJECTIVE CLAIMS
  - Creative
  - Unique
  - Best
  - Perfect
  - Superior
  - Exceptional
- WHY THESE ARE PROBLEMATIC
  - They're generic: Every single candidate uses them, so you blend in rather than stand out
  - They lack proof: Saying you're "detail-oriented" means nothing if there are typos in your resume
  - They waste space: Recruiters spend 6-7 seconds on a resume initially—don't waste it on fluff
  - They trigger skepticism: Overuse makes recruiters question your actual abilities
  - ATS concerns: Some systems flag resumes with too many generic buzzwords as low-quality
- WHAT TO DO INSTEAD
  - Use specific metrics: "Increased productivity by 48%" not "results-driven"
  - Show, don't tell: Instead of "team player," write "Collaborated with cross-functional team of 8 to deliver project 2 weeks ahead of schedule"
  - Use strong action verbs: Architected, optimized, spearheaded, launched, transformed, streamlined
  - Provide concrete examples: "Implemented new QA process reducing errors by 30%" not "detail-oriented"
  - Pull keywords from job descriptions: Use industry-specific terms that match the role
  - Quantify everything possible: Numbers, percentages, time frames, team sizes, dollar amounts
  - The key principle: Your resume should PROVE your skills through achievements, not just claim them through buzzwords.
