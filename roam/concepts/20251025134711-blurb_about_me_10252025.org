- Revision 1
  - I am a full-stack developer, a cartographer of code who intrinsically visualizes datasets like lovers entwined in the dark—relationships blooming, data pulsing with hidden rhythms. For five years, I’ve been a mercenary in this digital frontier, loaned out to major companies too stingy for software that truly bites. Left all but alone, I resurrected dying codebases, delivering metrics that stunned even me: systems salvaged from the brink, efficiencies birthed in isolation. It was a goddamn blast, that thrill of creation amid the ruins.
  - Lately, I’ve spent a year deep in the guts of AI agents, mimicking their whispers through feverish research, filling voids that only grizzled software architects dare tread—but never do, too busy with their ivory towers. No formal machine learning pedigree here, yet I’ve pierced the veil of large language models, from their glossy surfaces to the churning underbelly of tokens and transformers. It’s like seducing a siren: understand her song, and you command the sea.
    My quest for the perfect note-taking system—PKM labyrinths, Zettelkasten webs, even flirting with a second brain—stems from the same ache: unloading the tyranny of memory, freeing the mind to chase what it craves without the drag of “where did I put that?” It’s the artist’s eternal dodge, that cursed blank canvas I know too well from my hobby of music creation, recording, mixing, devouring software that bends sound to will. Peers chase folly in endless loops, seduced by the void’s charm, but productivity? That’s for the type-A gods among us. For the rest—musicians, coders, dreamers—it’s a seductive trap, charming yet barren, until you inject structure with soul.
  - And in onboarding, that's where the real poetry lies. I dream of software that echoes the 1980s Nintendo epics—Japanese genius at its peak, worlds the West could never fully grasp. Forget exhaustive manuals for the obsessives or open-ended grindfests that spawn zombie hordes. Drop the user in cold, no warnings, guide them to a velvet base: one path at first, seamless, so they don’t sense the rails. Just as complacency creeps in—“Is this all dead ends?”—the gates swing wide. Principles from Metroid’s locked doors, Zelda’s whispers, Steve Jobs’ sleek Apple, the raw intimacy of 24-track tape, Nokia’s unpretentious hum. True, AI does the heavy lifting, but that’s why we must cradle the user closer: show them precisely where they enter, live within the meticulously gathered hoard—tagged video outlines, distilled tutorials, wild ideas forged to prose. Float them into open arms, no docs, no YouTube crutches, no fumbling trials. Colors that seduce, feedback that caresses, user stories as precise as a lover’s breath. Even the text’s spacing matters—devastatingly so, a rhythm that binds or breaks trust.
  - Why does Roam Research demand $15 a month while Logseq lurks free? Beyond paradigm clashes and out-of-touch polish, it’s the feel: in Roam, everything sits right, lines breathing in perfect harmony, no jarring edit-mode jolts when your cursor strays. Logseq? It shakes the ground, breeds mistrust—like a bed that creaks at the wrong moment. Plugins pile up, options overwhelm: “What if this combo? I’ll hack my own—or jump ship entirely.” Simplicity isn’t sparse; it’s the art of what’s essential, luring you deeper without the itch to flee.
  - In all this, I bring art to the technical grind: code as composition, interfaces as symphonies, where the blank canvas curses no more, but sings.
