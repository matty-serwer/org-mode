** Technical Equipment & Skills
*** Audio Equipment
- *QSC TouchMix Series*
  - Compact, touchscreen‑driven digital mixers (e.g., TouchMix‑8/16/30) designed for small to mid‑sized productions where you may be both engineer and system tech.[1][2]
  - *Core concept*: Treat it like a “tablet with a full digital console inside” — most routing, EQ, and dynamics live on the main touch UI, with physical encoders for fine adjustments.[2][1]
  - *Workflow goals*:
    - Be able to build a basic show file quickly: patch inputs, name channels, set basic EQ/comp, and create at least 4–6 monitor mixes.
    - Use Simple vs Advanced modes: Simple for rapid deployment, Advanced when you need full control of EQ, dynamics, and routing.[3][2]
  - *Channel processing & tools*:
    - Every input has 4‑band fully parametric EQ, HPF/LPF, gate, and compressor; each output has parametric/graphic EQ, useful for ring‑out and system tuning.[1][2]
    - Built‑in “wizards” (Anti‑Feedback and Room Tuning) plus RTA help quickly stabilize PA and monitors without external analyzers.[2][3]
  - *Monitoring & mixes*:
    - Multiple Aux mixes (up to 10 on the 16) for wedges/IEMs; each mix can have its own processing and can be controlled from phones/tablets for musician self‑mix.[4][2]
    - Goal: Confidently flip between FOH mix and aux mixes without getting lost in the UI; know how to solo a bus and verify what you’re actually sending.
  - *Recording & playback*:
    - Direct multitrack recording to external USB drive plus stereo or multitrack playback for virtual soundcheck and post‑show content.[1][2]
    - Goal: Build a habit of capturing multitrack when allowed, so you can refine your mixes and create reference material.
- *Allen & Heath SQ‑5 Digital Mixer*
  - 48‑input, 96 kHz digital console built on the XCVI FPGA engine, with very low latency and a more “big‑desk” workflow than the TouchMix.[5][6]
  - *Surface & layers*:
    - 17 motorized faders (16 + master) across 6 layers; you can freely assign channels, DCAs, FX returns, and mixes to keep “live” things on the top layer.[7][5]
    - Goal: Standardize your own layer layout (e.g., Layer 1 = core inputs, Layer 2 = band inputs, Layer 3 = FX/returns/DCAs) so you can walk up to any SQ‑5 and feel oriented fast.
  - *Mix architecture*:
    - Up to 12 stereo mixes (auxes or groups) plus LR and 3 stereo matrices, giving plenty of outputs for PA, delays, fills, and IEMs.[5][7]
    - SLink port for remote stageboxes and a 64‑channel I/O port for Dante/other network audio when the rigs get more complex.[6][5]
  - *Processing & FX*:
    - Full channel strip on every input: HPF, 4‑band parametric EQ, compressor, gate; similar processing on mixes and matrices for system shaping.[8][6]
    - Eight stereo FX engines with dedicated returns; typical use is verbs, delays, and special FX without eating up input channels.[6][5]
  - *Control & apps*:
    - 7" capacitive touchscreen plus dedicated encoders and 8 assignable SoftKeys for mutes, tap tempo, scene recall, or FX toggles.[5][6]
    - SQ MixPad app for wireless control and offline show prep; SQ4You for performers to manage their own monitor sends.[7][5]
  - *Recording & redundancy*:
    - 32×32 USB interface for multitrack recording to DAW plus SQ‑Drive for direct recording to a USB stick — useful as a backup or for quick board mixes.[7][5]
    - Goal: Be comfortable setting up a show so you can simultaneously mix FOH, feed a livestream bus (post‑fader), and print a clean multitrack.
*** Video Equipment
- *Black Magic Design ATEM Switcher* (Critical – read the manual)
  - Goal: Be fluent with the ME structure, keying, and macro recalls by the second gig so you can call and cut a show confidently, not just “button push.”
  - Models range from 1‑ME to 4‑ME, giving multiple independent program/preview environments for different outputs.
  - *Mix Environment (ME) Concept*:
    - Think of each ME as a separate “video console” — a self‑contained mix layer that doesn’t automatically spill into others.
    - ME‑1 is typically your primary Program environment; background video must be in ME‑1, and cameras should all live in a single ME like a VCA bucket.
    - Sources/graphics assigned to an ME stay in that ME; if you need them elsewhere, they must be re‑routed or duplicated.
  - *Graphics & Alpha Feed*:
    - Use keyers and DSKs to layer graphics (titles, bugs, lower thirds) over camera mixes; one ME can be composited over another using key/fill (alpha) channels.
    - Cameras remain highest‑priority; graphics should never obscure critical action or client deliverables.
  - *Lower Thirds & Graphics*:
    - Build repeatable lower‑third templates with pre‑defined positions, safe‑title margins, and fade‑in/out behavior.
    - Use timers or macros so common elements (e.g., name straps, info ticker) auto‑fade after ~5 seconds instead of relying on manual kills.
*** Control Surfaces
- *Elgato Stream Deck*
  - Use with Bitfocus Companion or similar to create scene presets and macros for both ATEM and audio consoles (e.g., “Start Show”, “Break Bump”, “All Mute”).
- *Calling the Cameras*
  - Treat it as a separate responsibility: shot calling, anticipation of moves, and clear comms to ops so cuts feel intentional, not reactive.
