- Introduction To Crisis Intervention
  - Ken Kinter
  - Crisis
    - unexpected
    - threat to person and goals
    - must *change*
    - Characteristics
      - disorienting
      - internal or external
      - potential for danger
  - Stages of Crisis
    - Vulnerable
    - Active
    - Resolution
      - crisis resolution is prevention
  - Levels of Anxiety
    - Moderate
      - Sympathetic
    - Severe
      - Tunnel vision
      - focus irrelevant
    - Panic
      - Fight or Flight
      - Decreased ability to communicate
  - Recognizing a Crisis
  - Possible Outcomes
    - Suicidality
    - Aggressiveness
    - Homicidality
    - *RESOLUTION* - hopefully
  - Factors
    - Environmental
      - Volume
      - Space
      - People
      - Newness
    - Staff
      - Consistent and Familiar
      - Supportive as opposed to confrontational
    - Client
      - History of Violence?
      - Person's Diagnosis
        - substance
        - personality
        - psychosis
    - Situational
      - Recurring or singular?
      - What could be lost? How is that important?
      - What are they going to do?
      - Emotions
        - anger, fear, joy sadness, shame
  - Actions
    - Find previous solutions
      - What has worked in the past
      - "What usually helps you feel calm?"
      - "How have you managed in the past?"
    - Reinforce adaptive coping strategies
    - Recognizing that the client is dealing with extraordinary circumstances
    - Don't
      - "It will be ok"
      - "I know how you feel"
    - Talk to them about what they already know about solving their problems
    - Then help problem solve
    - Begin
      - working on what that person wants as a goal.
