* Core job titles
- *A1 – lead audio engineer*
  Primary mixer for the event; designs the PA, runs the console during show, and owns the overall sound experience.[2][5][1]
- *A2 – audio assistant / stage audio*
  Assists A1 with RF mics, IEMs, cable runs, comms, stage patching, quick fixes, and sometimes monitors.[4][5][1]
- *V1 – video engineer / switcher op*
  Designs and runs the video system: router, ATEM or other switcher, cameras, projectors/LED, and show record/stream feeds.[6][7][8][2][4]
- *V2 – video tech*
  Assists V1 with camera shading, cabling, projection, playback and general video setup/strike.[8][4]
- *L1 – lighting director/lead*
  Leads lighting system design, patching, programming and operation of the console.[3][5][8]
- *LD – lighting designer/director*
  Designs the look: cues, colors, focus, and runs or supervises the lighting console during the show.[5][3]
- *TD – technical director*
  Calls the show on comms, gives cues (standby… roll… fade…), and keeps audio, video, lighting, and stage in sync.[7][3][6]
- *Show caller / producer*
  Focuses on schedule, run‑of‑show, and communicating client needs to the technical crew.[3][6]
- *AV tech / utility / stagehand*
  Generalist who helps set, cable, label, strike, and solve problems across departments.[1][4][3]
-----

** Audio: consoles, positions, concepts
- *FOH (front of house)*
  Main audience mix position and the engineer working there; usually the A1 on these gigs.[1][3]
- *Monitors / MON world*
  Mix position and system for what talent hears on stage (wedges or IEMs).[3][1]
- *QSC TouchMix / Allen & Heath SQ-5*
  Digital live mixers used as FOH/monitor desks; scenes, layers, and buses are key concepts to learn on these.[2][1]
- *Bus / aux / subgroup / matrix*
  Different routing paths inside the console used for monitors, recordings, front fills, etc.[2][1]
- *RF coordination*
  Planning and tuning wireless mics/IEMs so frequencies don’t step on each other; often an A2 or specialist like “Carlos.”[5][1]
-----

** Video: switchers, M/Es, graphics
- *ATEM switcher*
  Blackmagic Design live video switcher used to cut between cameras, slides, and videos, and to key lower thirds.[9][10]
- *Program (PGM) vs Preview (PVW)*
  Program is what the audience/stream sees; preview is the “next shot” you’re lining up.[7][8]
- *M/E (mix/effects bus)*
  Independent mix layers on the switcher; each M/E can have its own program, keys, and transitions.[10][9]
- *Key / downstream key (DSK)*
  Layer used to put graphics (like lower thirds) on top of video, often using an alpha channel.[11][9][10]
- *Alpha channel / RGBA still*
  Graphics file with transparency, required for clean keyed lower thirds and bugs in ATEM media pool.[9][10][11]
- *Lower third*
  Name/ID bar near the bottom of the screen with fade‑in/fade‑out timing, often auto‑timed around 5 seconds.[10][11][9]
- *Graphics / GFX op*
  The person running slide software and other visual assets into the switcher.[4][8]
-----

** Control, macros, and automation
- *Elgato Stream Deck*
  Hardware button panel used to fire cues like camera cuts, graphics, and playback.[12][13][14]
- *Bitfocus Companion*
  Software that turns Stream Deck buttons into powerful macros that can hit multiple devices at once.[13][14][12]
- *Macro*
  A programmed sequence of actions (for example: cut to CAM 1, take lower third, fade it out after 5 seconds).[14][12][13]
- *Calling cameras*
  Giving clear, numbered instructions to camera ops over comms, often done by the TD or V1.[8][7]
-----

** Show flow and client-facing terms
- *Run of show (ROS)*
  Line‑by‑line schedule of cues: walk‑in, intros, keynotes, Q&A, walk‑out, etc.[6][7]
- *General session (GS)*
  Main big room where keynotes and major presentations happen.[5][8][3]
- *Breakout room*
  Smaller rooms for sessions that run alongside the general session.[8][3][5]
- *Strike*
  Tearing down and packing gear after the event ends.[4][1][3]
- *Client / talent / show site*
  Client = paying company, talent = presenters/performers, show site = venue where the event happens.[6][7][3]
