* 2022 Fender Player Jaguar Bass - Repair Notes
** Current Issues
- Bridge (jazz) pickup approximately 1/3 volume of middle (P-bass) pickup
- Jazz pickup volume drops steeply near maximum, becoming inaudible at full clockwise rotation
- Apparent phase issues causing sonic interference between pickups
- Symptoms consistent for over a year (not intermittent/dirty pots)
** Current Wiring Configuration
- Pickup Layout
  - *Neck position*: Jazz bass pickup
    - Yellow wire (hot) → Middle volume pot center lug
    - Black wire (ground) → Back of leftmost pot
  - *Middle position*: Split P-bass pickup (2 pickups wired together)
    - White wire → Middle volume pot
    - Ground → Back of P-bass volume pot
  - *Control layout*: 3 knobs (P-bass volume, Jazz volume, Tone/blend)
- Notable Wiring Details
  - Jack has only single wire connection (from tone pot middle lug to tip)
  - *No ground wire from jack sleeve* ⚠️
  - All grounds currently meeting at back of P-bass volume pot
  - Wiring matches factory 2022 Player Series schematic
- Comparison with Other Models
  - 2012 Jaguar Bass & Standard PJ Bass
    - Same pickup configuration (PJ with 3 knobs)
    - Different wiring scheme:
      - *Two wires to jack* (tip and sleeve)
      - Ground from jack sleeve → back of mixer/tone knob
      - Grounds meet at tone pot instead of volume pot
- Next Steps
  - *Acquire multimeter* for proper diagnostics
  - *Test pickup resistance* (Jazz should read ~7-8k ohms)
  - *Add ground wire* from jack sleeve to back of tone pot
  - *Consider rewiring* to match proven 2012 Jaguar/Standard PJ schematic
  - If issues persist, try reversing jazz pickup polarity (swap yellow/black)
- Potential Concerns
  - Double grounding when adding jack ground (likely not an issue)
  - Current factory wiring may be cost-reduced design
- Claude's Take
  - Primary Diagnosis
    - The missing jack ground is almost certainly your main problem
    - Without a proper ground reference at the jack, you're relying on the bridge ground through your body to complete the circuit
    - This creates an unstable, high-impedance ground path that can cause:
      - Volume drops and inconsistencies
      - Phase-like interference between pickups
      - The exact "fade to nothing at full volume" symptom you're experiencing
  - Why This Matters
    - The jazz pickup becoming inaudible at full volume is a telltale sign of improper grounding or reversed signal flow
    - When you turn the volume to maximum, you should have minimum resistance in the signal path
    - If it's cutting out completely, the signal is likely being shorted to ground through an incorrect path
  - The Wiring Evolution Theory
    - Fender likely "simplified" the wiring in newer Player Series models to reduce assembly time and cost
    - The older 2012 Jaguar and Standard PJ wiring schemes are time-tested and reliable
    - The fact that Fender changed away from a working design suggests cost-cutting rather than improvement
  - Recommended Approach
    - *Start with the jack ground* - This is the easiest fix and most likely culprit
    - *Use the multimeter* to verify pickup health before any drastic rewiring
    - *Document everything* with photos before making changes
    - *Consider full rewire* to 2012 spec if the simple fix doesn't work - it's a proven circuit
  - Additional Considerations
    - The "phase issues" you're hearing could actually be impedance mismatches from the poor grounding
    - Double grounding is generally not a problem - multiple ground paths are often beneficial
    - If you do reverse the jazz pickup polarity, mark which configuration you tried
    - Consider upgrading to CTS pots and switchcraft jack while you're in there (though this isn't necessary to fix your current issue)
    - The good news: This is almost certainly a wiring issue, not a bad pickup
    - The pickups themselves are likely fine, and this should be completely fixable with some patient troubleshooting
