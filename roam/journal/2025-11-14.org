*** Living and Working at the Hostel in Skopje, North Macedonia

  - Formed strong connections and friendships in Skopje after earlier travels and Brad's prior years in the region.
- Became involved with a group running a hostel in Skopje, initially named Hostel Hostel (a pun referencing European horror films).
  - Invested in a new Skopje location with three partners.
    - Old location converted into a communal event space.
    - Transformed a large, multi-level house in Skopje into the new hostel by remodeling bedrooms and utility rooms for guests.
- Hostel operations and management were informal; anyone present took on management duties as needed.
  - Over two years, was the most consistently involved partner as others would come and go.
    - Brad and Naza (who dated on and off) alternated time spent at the hostel.
    - Frequently managed operations with partner Pero.
- Responsibilities and daily activities included:
  - Checking guests in and out, collecting passport information, and handling registration legally.
  - Preparing daily breakfasts and lunches—simple, local meals, sometimes with special touches.
  - Managing laundry and housekeeping.
  - Entertaining guests: impromptu tours, visits to casinos and dance clubs, local sightseeing.
- Focused on building a welcoming, social environment:
  - Used group social skills and personality to build the hostel's reputation through word-of-mouth.
  - Relished showing guests highlights of Skopje and quickly integrating them into the local scene.
  - Helped travelers who were often passing through Skopje as a waystation between countries (e.g., Poland, Greece, Turkey, Vienna).
    - Provided a respite from less welcoming hostels in other countries, ensuring guests felt cared for.
- The new location was officially named "Happiness Is The Meaning Of Life," commonly abbreviated as HITMOL for business, letterhead, and signage.
  - Name's abbreviation (HITMOL) was sometimes seen as limiting—occasionally wished the full name was always used.
- Approach to hospitality:
  - Saw entertainment and outings as meaningful guest service and personal enjoyment.
  - Believed it was essential to actively help guests make the most of a short stay, rather than see them struggle in a new city.
  - Many guests extended their stays, arriving for two days but staying a week or longer, drawn by the casual, concierge-like atmosphere.
