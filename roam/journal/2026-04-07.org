      - Assign the Global Administrator role to the new account

    - *Using the account*
      - Technician logs into all required portals using the temp account
      - Account should not be used outside the scope of the engagement
    - *Remove the account*
      - Log into [[admin.microsoft.com][admin.microsoft.com]] using the tenant owner's credentials
      - Go to Users > Active Users
      - Select the temp admin account
      - Click Delete user and confirm
      - Go to Billing > Licenses and confirm the license has been unassigned and is available
      - Verify the account no longer appears under Users or Roles

- *The Unsupported Path: Jung's Framework for Building Psychological Autonomy*
  -----

  *Key Vocabulary Glossary*
  Term Definition *Scaffolding* External social/institutional structures that support and construct identity *Inherited self* A sense of self assembled gradually from others' approval and validation *Shadow* Jung's term for the unconscious sum of everything the psyche has refused to integrate *Individuation* Jung's process of psychological development toward wholeness and genuine selfhood *Solitude vs. Loneliness* Conscious interior encounter (solitude) vs. passive suffering of isolation (loneliness) *Heroic isolation* The unconscious reframing of emotional unavailability as strength or ideology *Psychological autonomy* A stable sense of self that does not require external validation to function *Necessity* The condition Jung identifies as the true engine of personality development
  - *Core premise:* The absence of external support, when confronted consciously, can accelerate psychological development in ways that supported paths rarely achieve
  - *I. The Pain of an Unequal Starting Line*
    - The unnamed feeling of watching others succeed with structural advantages (connections, mentors, family backing)
    - Same ambition, different conditions — not failure, not jealousy, but something more precise
    - The suppressed question: /What if certain things are genuinely impossible without support?/
    - Key reframe: this is not a resource problem — it's an *identity problem* wearing the mask of one
  - *II. How Most People Build a Self (Without Knowing It)*
    - *Inherited self:* The self most people carry was assembled gradually through external validation — parental approval, institutional recognition, others' belief
    - This process is normal and functional, but it produces a self whose /foundations run outward, not inward/
    - *The hidden cost of scaffolding:* external support accelerates progress but also removes the pressure that forces genuine interior development
    - Key vocabulary: *scaffolding* (the social/institutional structures that hold identity in place)
  - *III. Jung's Break with Freud as Case Study*
    - In 1913, Jung lost his most powerful professional validator and the institutional framework that gave his work legitimacy
    - Forced into isolation, he underwent what he called a *confrontation with the unconscious* — a period of profound disorientation
    - What emerged was a self that knew who it was /because it had been forced to find out without anyone's help/
    - His subsequent work became the foundation of analytical psychology
  - *IV. The Psychology of Necessity*
    - Jung's central claim: *genuine personality develops through necessity, not comfort or encouragement*
    - The psyche is conservative — it will not do the hard interior work unless it /must/
    - Without external structure to fall back on, the psyche faces a forced choice: go inward or collapse
    - Key vocabulary:
      - *Individuation* — Jung's term for the process by which the psyche moves toward wholeness, integrating what has been rejected, developing what has been undeveloped
      - *Psychological autonomy* — a sense of self that does not depend on external confirmation to remain stable
  - *V. The Shadow and the Silence*
    - *The shadow* — Jung's term for everything the psyche has refused to integrate: repressed fears, unlived potential, the version of you buried under performed identity
    - Noise (busyness, social obligations, endless distraction) functions as a /buffer/ that keeps the shadow encounter theoretical
    - Those without support have a thinner buffer — the silence arrives sooner and forces an earlier confrontation
    - *Loneliness vs. Solitude:*
      - *Loneliness* — passive suffering of isolation; what happens when you resist being alone
      - *Solitude* — the conscious choice to remain in interior encounter; the condition under which transformation becomes possible
    - The unsupported person's driving force is often /defiance/, not desire — the need to prove wrong those who didn't show up. This is a wound pretending to be an engine
  - *VI. The Two Traps*
    - *Trap 1: Victimization*
      - Begins as honest acknowledgment that circumstances are harder without support (which is true)
      - Calcifies into identity: /therefore it is impossible for me/
      - Bitterness functions as a closed system — everything incoming becomes evidence of the original wound
      - Protects against the vulnerability of public failure, but forecloses possibility in the same motion
    - *Trap 2: Heroic Isolation (the Heroic Refusal)*
      - The decision — usually unconscious — to reframe /not needing anyone/ as a superpower rather than a wound
      - Independence becomes ideology; solitude becomes armor
      - Sophisticated avoidance of grief, risk, and the admission that connection is a genuine human requirement
      - Jung himself, during his period of isolation, maintained real anchors (family, clinical work) — he did not romanticize solitude
      - *Defended is not the same as developed*
    - *What both traps share:* they bypass the actual interior work — one by making the world fully responsible, the other by making the self fully sufficient. Both are fictions.
    - The narrow path between them requires holding two things in tension simultaneously:
      - The genuine injustice of your circumstances
      - The genuine agency you still possess within them
      - /That tension is not a problem to solve — it is the condition under which the real work happens/
  - *VII. What the Unsupported Path Actually Builds*
    - Supported paths build faster but produce a self whose foundations can be /dismantled from the outside/, because they were constructed from the outside
    - What sustained unsupported effort produces (unglamorous, unwitnessed):
      - *Autonomous judgment* — the repeated proof that your decisions can function without external endorsement
      - *Demonstrated resilience* — the lived evidence that you can be broken by circumstances and continue anyway
      - *Signal location* — the capacity to find your own direction in the absence of external noise
    - Jung's description of the developed personality: cannot be shaken by collective panic, cannot be reoriented by withdrawal of approval, cannot be collapsed by removal of external structure — /because it is not resting on any of those things/
    - Key asymmetry: "They are building faster. You are building differently."
  - *VIII. The Condition That Makes It Matter: Consciousness*
    - Pressure alone does not produce individuation — it produces either a forged self or a fractured one
    - The difference is *consciousness* — the willingness to understand what the process is actually doing and navigate it with intention rather than merely endure it
    - Endurance is not the destination; it is the starting condition
  - *IX. The Final Reframe*
    - The self that emerges from genuine necessity — locating your own values, direction, and worth without external confirmation — is the only self that is /fully yours/
    - Not inherited, not borrowed, not contingent on conditions that can change
    - Most people never find out who they are in this sense, not from weakness, but because the scaffolding never fully came down
    - What the pressure does not create — it /reveals/
    - "The only kind of life that cannot be taken from you is the one that was never given to you in the first place"
