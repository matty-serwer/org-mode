  - TP‑Link AC1200 range extenders (like RE305/RE205) are designed to connect to any existing Wi‑Fi network in “Range Extender” mode, then rebroadcast it under an “_EXT” network name.[3][4]
  - In principle, your iPhone hotspot is just another Wi‑Fi network, so the extender can join it the same way it would join a normal router SSID.[5][6]
  1. Go to Settings → Personal Hotspot.
  1. Turn on “Allow Others to Join” and set a strong password.
  1. If available, enable “Maximize Compatibility” (this usually forces 2.4 GHz, which extenders tend to prefer).[7]
  1. Plug the extender into an outlet close to your iPhone and wait for solid power LED.[3]
  1. On your MacBook or phone, join the default TP‑Link extender Wi‑Fi (e.g., “TP‑Link_Extender” or similar).[4][8]
  1. Open the TP‑Link Tether app or go to [[http://tplinkrepeater.net][http://tplinkrepeater.net]] in a browser, create/login to a local admin password if asked.[8][9]
  1. Choose “Range Extender” / “Extend existing Wi‑Fi,” scan for networks, and select the iPhone hotspot SSID, then enter its password.[9][10][4]
  1. Let it finish; it will create an extended network (e.g., “YourHotspot_EXT”).
  1. Connect your MacBook to that extended network.
  - iPhone or carrier limiting how hotspot traffic is routed through another NAT device (like an extender).[2][1]
  - IP/subnet quirks where the extender and hotspot end up in incompatible address ranges.[6]
  - Turn hotspot off and back on, then power‑cycle the extender and redo the quick setup.

  - *USB tether to MacBook, then share from Mac:*
    Plug iPhone into your MacBook with a cable, enable Personal Hotspot → “USB only” or allow USB, then on the Mac use System Settings → Sharing → Internet Sharing to share that connection over Wi‑Fi; your Mac becomes the *access point* for other devices.
  - *Use a small travel router that supports phone tethering or hotspot repeating:*
    Some mini routers are explicitly designed to repeat mobile hotspots or use iPhone USB tethering as WAN, which tends to be more reliable than using a generic extender in this scenario.[12][5]
