Here’s a best-effort “actual soundtrack” tracklist for Trainspotting, in film order, with each track’s original or most-associated *non‑Trainspotting* release. I’ll flag where sources disagree or where the match is probabilistic.
-----

** 1. Lust for Life – Iggy Pop
- Film use: Opening chase, Renton and Spud running from the police.[1][2]
- Primary source release:
  - Album: /Lust for Life/ (1977 studio album) by Iggy Pop.[1]
-----

** 2. Carmen: Habanera – Georges Bizet
- Film use: Renton prepares for withdrawal; calls Mikey Forrester for opium suppositories.[2]
- Primary source release:
  - Work: /Carmen/, Act I, aria “L’amour est un oiseau rebelle (Habanera)”.
  - The specific recording in some listings is by Orquesta Sinfónica de Radio Praga conducted by Pietro di La Coronna, but the canonical “source” is Bizet’s opera /Carmen/ (1875).[functions.fetch_url:0][1]
-----

** 3. Deep Blue Day – Brian Eno
- Film use: Renton dives into the toilet to retrieve the opium suppositories.[2]
- Primary source release:
  - Album: /Apollo: Atmospheres and Soundtracks/ (1983) by Brian Eno (with Daniel Lanois & Roger Eno).[1]
-----

** 4. Trainspotting – Primal Scream
- Film use: Park scene with Renton and Sick Boy shooting BB guns and the dog incident.[2]
- Primary source release:
  - Originally a standalone single / non‑album track recorded specifically around the time; its key early release is the “Trainspotting” single and later the Primal Scream compilation /Dirty Hits/ (2003). The “original” is the 1994–95 single, not created as a score cue.[1]
-----

** 5. Temptation – Heaven 17
- Film use: Club scene where Renton arrives and dances; Spud and Tommy talk at a table.[2]
- Primary source release:
  - Album: /Penthouse and Pavement/ (1981) – the band’s debut, later re-recorded, but the commonly associated original full‑band synthpop version is the 1983 single and album‑era recording.[1]
-----

** 6. Atomic – Blondie / Sleeper
There’s a split between “in‑film original” and “OST cover” here.
- Film use (on-screen): Lists of film music show the /original/ “Atomic” by Blondie as what appears in the film’s diegesis.[1]
- Soundtrack album: The OST uses Sleeper’s cover version “Atomic” (produced by Stephen Street).[1]
- Primary source release you asked for (original, non‑Trainspotting):
  - Original: Blondie – “Atomic” from the album /Eat to the Beat/ (1979) and released as a single in 1980.[1]
  - Cover: Sleeper – “Atomic” (Trainspotting‑era single and b‑side; later compilations), but its “home” release is basically the single itself, not a prior album.[1]
Given your brief (“appears elsewhere previous to the release of the film”), the Blondie original on /Eat to the Beat/ is the canonical source.
-----

** 7. Temptation – New Order
- Film use: Dinner scene with Diane’s parents; Renton realises he has slept with a minor.[2]
- Complete-film list: Identifies the track simply as “Temptation – New Order”.[1]
- Versions background:
  - 1982 original single (7" and 12") on Factory; 12" version later on the EP /1981–1982/.[3]
  - 1987 re-recording for the compilation /Substance 1987/ (often labeled “Temptation ’87” in later releases).[3]
- Which version in the film?
  - The general consensus in fan and discographic notes is that the /Substance/‑era re‑recording (“Temptation ’87”) is the one used in Trainspotting, and that its subsequent prominence is due to the OST.[4][3]
- Primary source release (non‑Trainspotting):
  - Compilation album: /Substance 1987/ by New Order (1987); track often retitled “Temptation ’87” in reissues.[3]
So for our list: use the /Substance 1987/ version as the “most associated” pre‑film source.
-----

** 8. Nightclubbing – Iggy Pop
- Film use: Heroin-shooting scene in the flat.[2]
- Primary source release:
  - Album: /The Idiot/ (1977) by Iggy Pop.[1]
-----

** 9. Sing – Blur
- Film use: Shoplifting/police chase; Renton gets hit by a car; trial lead‑up.[2]
- Primary source release:
  - Album: /Leisure/ (1991) by Blur.[1]
-----

** 10. Perfect Day – Lou Reed
- Film use: Renton’s overdose and hospital sequence.[2]
- Primary source release:
  - Album: /Transformer/ (1972) by Lou Reed.[1]
-----

** 11. Dark & Long (Dark Train) – Underworld
- Film use: Withdrawals sequence with hallucinations (Diane singing, Dawn on the ceiling, TV game show).[2]
- Primary source release:
  - EP/Single: “Dark & Long (Dark Train)” was released as a separate 12" / remix single derived from the /Dubnobasswithmyheadman/ era (~1994), and appears on Underworld’s single/EP releases compiling “Dark Train”.[2]
  - Parent album for the base track “Dark & Long”: /Dubnobasswithmyheadman/ (1994).[2]
-----

** 12. Think About the Way – Ice MC
- Film use: Renton goes to London and works as a letting agent.[2]
- Primary source release:
  - Album: /Ice’n’Green/ (1994) by Ice MC; the track is also a 1994 single.[1]
-----

** 13. Mile End – Pulp
- Film use: Begbie arrives to stay in Renton’s tiny London flat.[2]
- Complete-film list notes that the version is “Mile End – From ‘Trainspotting’ Original Motion Picture Soundtrack” for the specific master, but the song itself predates the film.[2]
- Primary source release:
  - Originally a b‑side to the single “Common People” (1995) and later included on Pulp compilation releases; its first association is that 1995 single rather than the OST.[1]
-----

** 14. For What You Dream Of (Full On Renaissance Mix) – Bedrock feat. KYO
- Film use: Renton and Begbie at the club; Renton narrates about the changing drug culture.[2]
- Primary source release:
  - 12" single: “For What You Dream Of” (Bedrock featuring KYO), Full On Renaissance Mix, first released on Guerrilla Records (early 1990s), and was heavily associated with the /Renaissance: The Mix Collection/ era of club compilations.[1]
-----

** 15. 2:1 – Elastica
- Film use: Sick Boy moves into Renton’s apartment; the TV has been sold.[2]
- Primary source release:
  - Album: /Elastica/ (1995) by Elastica.[1]
-----

** 16. Herzlich Tut Mich Verlangen – Gábor Lehotka
- Film use: Organ music at Tommy’s funeral.[2]
- Primary source release:
  - This is a Lutheran chorale (“Herzlich tut mich verlangen”), best known from Bach’s harmonisations (e.g., chorale BWV 727), and often recorded under that title.
  - The specific performance credited is organist Gábor Lehotka; discographic data points to recordings of Bach chorales/organ works on classical organ recital albums prior to the film.[functions.fetch_url:0][1]
-----

** 17. A Final Hit – Leftfield
- Film use: Renton tests the heroin before taking it to London to sell to the Russians.[2]
- Primary source release:
  - Initially a non‑album track by Leftfield; pre‑film it appears on singles/promos associated with the /Leftism/ era (mid‑1990s) and then on /Trainspotting/ OST and later Leftfield compilations.
  - Since you want a non‑Trainspotting anchor: its earliest non‑film association is as a b‑side/extra track in Leftfield’s mid‑90s single catalogue, but there is not a strongly canonical pre‑Trainspotting album home; it effectively debuts in the soundtrack ecosystem.[1]
So here we have to accept this is one of the few pieces that is “practically” born with the film context, though not a traditional score cue.
-----

** 18. Statuesque – Sleeper
- Film use: Pub celebration after the successful drug deal with the Russians.[2]
- Primary source release:
  - Album: /The It Girl/ (1996) by Sleeper.[1]
-----

** 19. Born Slippy .NUXX – Underworld
- Film use: Final heist/walk‑away sequence where Renton takes the money and vows to “choose life”.[2]
- Primary source release:
  - Originally the b‑side “Born Slippy .NUXX” to the single “Born Slippy” (1995) on Junior Boy’s Own; it later appears on the compilation /1965–1995/ and on Underworld’s /Second Toughest in the Infants/ reissues, but its original home is that 1995 single.[1]
-----

** 20. Closet Romantic – Damon Albarn
- Film use: End credits.[2]
- Primary source release:
  - Non‑album track recorded by Damon Albarn around the /Blur///The Great Escape/ era, first widely available via the Trainspotting OST and later some compilations.
  - There’s no strongly documented, earlier album home; it’s essentially a standalone composition that surfaces with the film tie‑in.[1]
-----

** Additional songs appearing only on Trainspotting #2 or related releases
These are associated with the film universe but not actually heard in the movie according to the “Complete film soundtrack” list.[1]
- “Choose Life” – PF Project feat. Ewan McGregor (club track built around the “Choose life” monologue; originally a 1997 single).[1]
- “The Passenger” – Iggy Pop, from /Lust for Life/ (1977).[1]
- “Golden Years” – David Bowie, from /Station to Station/ (1976).[1]
- “Our Lips Are Sealed” – Fun Boy Three, from /Fun Boy Three/ (1982).[1]
- “Come Together” – Primal Scream, original version from /Screamadelica/ (1991).[1]
- “Atmosphere” – Joy Division, originally a 1980 single (later on /Substance/).[1]
- “Inner City Life” – Goldie, from /Timeless/ (1995).[1]
They’re not in the film’s on‑screen music chronology but are sometimes considered part of the broader Trainspotting sound world.
